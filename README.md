**2026 Annual Meeting of the East Lakes Division of the American Association of Geographers, Columbus, Ohio**

**Where Does Abandoned Cropland Go?: A Decade of Land-Cover Trajectories in Ohio**

**Sanghoon Ji, Sohyun Park* (Computational and Data Sciences, George Mason University Korea)**

**Abstract**: 
Crop abandonment is not an end state but a transition with several pathways. Yet large-scale studies describe what abandoned land becomes with a single land-cover snapshot or assess its future against two predefined uses, recultivation or reforestation, simplifying the process. We test whether the nine factors from a global model of recultivation suitability, rebuilt with U.S. data, also distinguish post-abandonment trajectories. In Ohio, which spans the Corn Belt and the Appalachian Plateau, we tracked 30 m cropland pixels abandoned in 2001–2014 through Annual NLCD from five years before to ten years after abandonment and clustered them into three main trajectory types: Cropland Persistence, Pasture/Hay Transition, and Urban Transition. Spatial error and lag models related each type's share in 300 m grid cells to these factors. Cropland Persistence dominated the west, most strongly associated with surrounding cropland density and proximity to stable cropland. Pasture/Hay Transition dominated the east and south, and was most strongly associated with the density of nearby abandoned cropland. Treating abandonment as trajectories reveals pathways that snapshot framings might miss. In Ohio, these pathways suggest that the end of crop cultivation often marks a shift within agricultural land use rather than an exit from it.
